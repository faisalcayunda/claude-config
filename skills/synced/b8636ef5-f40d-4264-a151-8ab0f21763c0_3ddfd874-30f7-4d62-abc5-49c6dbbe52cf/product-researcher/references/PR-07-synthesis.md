# PR-06: Trend Analysis & Foresight

## Technology Adoption Lifecycle (Moore)
Innovators → Early Adopters → **[CHASM]** → Early Majority → Late Majority → Laggards
**Crossing strategy**: Dominate one beachhead niche with "whole product" before expanding.

## Gartner Hype Cycle — 5 Phases
Technology Trigger → Peak of Inflated Expectations → Trough of Disillusionment → Slope of Enlightenment → Plateau of Productivity
**STREET framework** (Fenn/Raskino): Scope, Tracking record, Risks, Effort, Economic viability, Timeframe

## Scenario Planning (Schwartz — 8 steps)
1. Identify focal issue or decision
2. Identify key forces in the local environment
3. Identify driving forces (macro trends)
4. Rank by importance and uncertainty
5. Select scenario logics (2×2 matrix of top uncertainties)
6. Flesh out scenarios (narratives)
7. Determine implications
8. Select leading indicators and signposts

## Signal Detection (Webb)
**Signal** = early indicator of emerging change. Not yet a trend.
**Trend** = pattern of change driven by converging signals.
Process: Scan edges → Identify signals → Map convergences → Name trends → Assess timing (inflection point analysis)

## Superforecasting (Tetlock)
Key practices: Break questions into components, use base rates, update incrementally (Bayesian thinking), distinguish signal from noise. Superforecasters outperform domain experts by **60%+** on geopolitical forecasting.

# PR-07: Research Synthesis & Insight Generation

## Thematic Analysis — 6 Phases (Braun & Clarke 2006)
1. **Familiarize** — Read/re-read transcripts, note initial observations
2. **Generate codes** — Label meaningful data segments systematically
3. **Search for themes** — Group codes into candidate themes
4. **Review themes** — Check themes against coded extracts and full dataset
5. **Define & name** — Refine each theme's scope and essence
6. **Write up** — Select compelling extracts, produce analytical narrative

## Atomic Research (Sharon 2017)
Four atom types: **Experiment → Fact → Insight → Recommendation**
Each insight traceable to specific evidence (video clips, quotes, data points).
Store in research repository → tag → make searchable → democratize access.

## Affinity Diagramming (Holtzblatt/Beyer)
1. Write each observation on individual notes (one idea per note)
2. Bottom-up clustering: silently group similar notes
3. Create labels for each cluster (these become themes)
4. Identify hierarchy: themes → sub-themes → observations
5. Use color coding: e.g., blue = behavior, green = pain, yellow = opportunity

## Insight Writing — Pyramid Principle (Anderson-Stanier)
Structure: **Insight statement** (so what?) → **Supporting evidence** (3-5 data points) → **Recommendation** (what to do)
Good insight: Specific, actionable, surprising, supported by evidence from multiple sources (triangulation).

## Triangulation (Denzin)
| Type | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Data** | Multiple data sources (interviews + analytics + surveys) |
| **Investigator** | Multiple researchers analyzing same data |
| **Theory** | Multiple theoretical frameworks interpreting data |
| **Methodological** | Multiple methods (qual + quant) |

# PR-08: Pricing Research

## Van Westendorp PSM — 4 Questions
1. "At what price would it be **so cheap** you'd question quality?"
2. "At what price is it a **bargain** — great buy for the money?"
3. "At what price is it **getting expensive** but you'd still consider?"
4. "At what price is it **too expensive** to consider?"
Plot cumulative curves → intersection points define:
- **Point of Marginal Cheapness** (PMC)
- **Optimal Price Point** (OPP)
- **Indifference Price Point** (IDP)
- **Point of Marginal Expensiveness** (PME)
Acceptable range: PMC to PME

## Gabor-Granger Technique
Present price sequentially (randomize starting direction):
"Would you buy at $X?" → Yes → increase price → "At $Y?" → ... until No
Produces: Individual price sensitivity curve → demand curve → revenue-maximizing price

## Conjoint for Pricing (Orme)
Include price as one attribute alongside features. CBC (Choice-Based Conjoint) produces:
- Part-worth utilities for each price level
- Willingness-to-pay for each feature
- Market simulation: predict share at any price/feature combination

## 9 Rules of Monetization (Ramanujam 2016)
1. Have the WTP conversation EARLY (before product design)
2. Don't default to one-size-fits-all
3. Configuration not customization
4. Choose the right pricing model (subscription, usage, tiered)
5. Price on value, not cost
6. Develop Good/Better/Best options
7. Know the 4 failure types (Feature Shock, Minivation, Hidden Gem, Undead)
8. Build an ROI model
9. Test pricing as rigorously as features

# PR-09: Voice of Customer Programs

## VoC Methodology (Griffin & Hauser 1993)
- **20-30 interviews capture ~90% of customer needs**
- Needs expressed in customer's own words, not company jargon
- Hierarchical structure: strategic needs → tactical needs → operational needs
- Cross-functional teams should participate in hearing customer voices

## NPS 3.0 (Reichheld 2021)
| Component | Definition |
|:---|:---|
| **NPS** | % Promoters − % Detractors (survey-based) |
| **Earned Growth Rate** | Revenue from returning customers + referrals (accounting-based) |
Together: NPS measures sentiment, EGR measures behavior. Companies with dual tracking grow sustainably.

## 6 Ds Framework (Qualtrics/Temkin)
Detect → Disseminate → Diagnose → Discuss → Design → Deploy
Mature VoC programs close the loop: feedback → insight → action → communication back to customer.

## Customer Advisory Board (Gospe)
- **12 strategically selected customers** (not just advocates)
- Third-party facilitation for candor
- **80/20 listening ratio** — no product demos or sales
- Quarterly or biannual cadence
- Share strategic direction, get unvarnished feedback

# PR-10: Research Operations

## ResearchOps Framework (Towsey 2024)
8 elements: Environment → Scope → People → Governance → Recruitment → Data → Ethics → Tooling
**Planning Matrix**: Maps activities × roles × frequency for operationalizing research at scale.

## Participant Recruitment Best Practices
- Build and maintain a research panel (opt-in, diverse)
- Screen for relevant experience, not just demographics
- Offer fair incentives ($50-150/hr for B2B, $20-50 for consumer)
- Over-recruit by 20% for no-shows
- Maintain a CRM for past participants (avoid "research fatigue")

## GDPR Compliance in Research
| Requirement | Research Application |
|:---|:---|
| **Lawful basis** | Consent (Article 6) — explicit, informed, withdrawable |
| **Data minimization** | Collect only what's needed for research objectives |
| **Purpose limitation** | Use data only for stated research purpose |
| **Storage limitation** | Delete recordings after analysis unless consent for longer |
| **Right to erasure** | Participant can request deletion at any time |

## Research Democratization (NNg, Sirjani)
**9 Pillars** (Sirjani): Shared language, tooling, training, templates, quality checks, repository, research support, ethical guidelines, feedback loops.
Key distinction: **Professional Researchers** (design studies, complex analysis) vs **PwDR — People who do research** (run templated interviews, use tools with guardrails).
