# PR-01: Market Research Methodology

## Malhotra's 6-Step Research Process
1. **Define** the problem and research objectives
2. **Develop** the research approach (exploratory/descriptive/causal)
3. **Design** the research (methodology, sampling, instruments)
4. **Collect** the data (fieldwork, quality control)
5. **Analyze** the data (statistical techniques, interpretation)
6. **Report** findings (presentation, recommendations)

## Research Types
| Type | Purpose | Methods | When |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Exploratory** | Understand the problem space | Interviews, ethnography, diary studies | Early discovery |
| **Descriptive** | Quantify attitudes/behaviors | Surveys, analytics, benchmarks | Validating hypotheses |
| **Causal** | Establish cause-effect | Experiments, A/B tests | Testing solutions |

## Sampling Approaches (Lohr)
| Category | Methods | Use |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Probability** | Simple random, stratified, cluster | Generalizable findings |
| **Non-probability** | Convenience, snowball, purposive | Exploratory, qualitative |
| Rule of thumb | 20-30 interviews for qualitative saturation (Griffin/Hauser) |

## ESOMAR Code — Key Principles
- Informed consent and voluntary participation
- Distinction between research and sales/marketing
- Data protection and participant privacy
- Transparency about methodology
- Professional competence requirements

# PR-02: Customer Discovery & Interviews

## The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick) — 3 Rules
1. **Talk about their life**, not your idea
2. **Ask about specifics** in the past, not opinions about the future
3. **Talk less, listen more** — they should be doing most of the talking

Good: "Tell me about the last time you tried to [problem]..."
Bad: "Would you use a product that does [your idea]?"

## Customer Development (Blank) — 4 Steps
1. **Customer Discovery** — "Do people have this problem?"
2. **Customer Validation** — "Will they pay for our solution?"
3. **Customer Creation** — "How do we scale demand?"
4. **Company Building** — "How do we build the organization?"

## Opportunity Solution Tree (Torres 2021)
```
Desired Outcome (OKR Key Result)
├── Opportunity 1 (unmet need/pain/desire)
│   ├── Solution A → Assumption Test
│   └── Solution B → Assumption Test
├── Opportunity 2
│   └── Solution C → Experiment
```
Weekly rhythm: Customer interview → Update tree → Run assumption tests

## JTBD Switch Interview (Moesta) — 4 Forces
| Force | Direction | Question |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Push** | Away from current | "What was going wrong that made you look for something new?" |
| **Pull** | Toward new solution | "What did you hope the new solution would do for you?" |
| **Anxiety** | Resists change | "What concerns did you have about switching?" |
| **Habit** | Resists change | "What kept you using the old solution for so long?" |

## Interview Techniques (Portigal, Hansen)
- **Silence**: Pause after answers — interviewees fill silence with deeper thoughts
- **Echo**: Repeat their last phrase as a question — "Frustrating?"
- **Laddering**: "Why?" chain to uncover root motivations
- **Critical incident**: "Tell me about a specific time when..."
- **Projective**: "If you could wave a magic wand..."

# PR-03: Survey Design & Quantitative Research

## Tailored Design Method (Dillman)
Based on **social exchange theory**: maximize perceived reward, minimize perceived cost, build trust.
- Personalized contact, clear purpose, reasonable length
- Multiple contacts: pre-notice → questionnaire → reminder → thank you
- Result: 60%+ response rates achievable

## Question Design (Krosnick/Presser)
**Satisficing theory**: Respondents take cognitive shortcuts when questions are hard, motivation is low, or ability is limited.
Countermeasures: Simple language, avoid double-barreled questions, use balanced scales, randomize option order.

## Key Survey Types for Product Research
| Survey | Author | Core Question | Threshold |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **PMF Survey** | Ellis | "How would you feel if you could no longer use [X]?" | ≥40% "very disappointed" |
| **NPS** | Reichheld | "How likely to recommend?" (0-10) | >50 excellent |
| **CES** | Dixon | "How easy was it to [task]?" (1-7) | Lower effort = higher loyalty |
| **CSAT** | General | "How satisfied are you?" (1-5) | >4.0 good |

## Conjoint & MaxDiff (Orme/Sawtooth)
**Conjoint**: Shows product profiles with varied attributes → reveals relative importance and part-worth utilities. Best for pricing + feature tradeoff research.
**MaxDiff**: Shows sets of items → respondents pick best/worst → produces ratio-scaled preference rankings. Better discrimination than rating scales.

# PR-04: Competitive Intelligence & Market Sizing

## Porter's Five Forces (for Research)
| Force | Research Questions |
|:---|:---|
| **Rivalry** | Who are the direct competitors? How differentiated? Price competition? |
| **New Entrants** | What are barriers to entry? Who's funding startups in this space? |
| **Substitutes** | What alternatives do customers use? What "non-consumption" exists? |
| **Buyer Power** | How price-sensitive are buyers? Switching costs? |
| **Supplier Power** | Key dependencies? Platform lock-in? |

## Win/Loss Analysis (Clozd — 4 Pillars)
1. **Program Design**: Third-party interviews (not internal), 15-20 min per interview
2. **Data Collection**: Buyer interviews within 30 days of decision
3. **Analysis**: Pattern identification across deals — themes, not anecdotes
4. **Action**: Quarterly reports → sales training + product roadmap input
Key finding: **84% of companies** conducting win/loss 2+ years report increased win rates.

## Market Sizing — TAM/SAM/SOM
| Level | Definition | Method |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **TAM** | Total Addressable Market | Top-down: Industry reports, analyst data |
| **SAM** | Serviceable Available Market | TAM filtered by geography, segment, channel |
| **SOM** | Serviceable Obtainable Market | SAM × realistic market share (1-5 year) |
| **Bottom-up** | Customer count × ARPU | More credible for investors |

## CI Framework (Fuld, Gilad, SCIP)
Ethical CI cycle: Planning → Collection → Analysis → Dissemination → Feedback
**FAROUT** evaluation (Fleisher/Bensoussan): Future-oriented, Accurate, Resource-efficient, Objective, Useful, Timely

# PR-05: Product-Market Fit Research

## PMF Measurement — Multi-Signal Approach
| Signal | Method | PMF Threshold |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Ellis Survey** | "How disappointed?" | ≥40% "very disappointed" |
| **Retention Curve** | Cohort analysis | Curve flattens above 0% |
| **Organic Growth** | Word-of-mouth tracking | Growing without paid acquisition |
| **NPS** | Recommendation likelihood | >40 |
| **Qualitative** | Interview depth | Users describe product as "must-have" |

## Vohra's Superhuman PMF Engine (5 steps)
1. Survey users with Ellis question + "What type of people would benefit most?"
2. Segment by "very disappointed" persona
3. Analyze what fans love (double down) and what holds back fence-sitters (fix)
4. Build roadmap: 50% delight fans / 50% convert fence-sitters
5. Track PMF score weekly; Superhuman went **22% → 58%**

## PMF Pyramid (Olsen)
Bottom → Top: Target Customer → Underserved Needs → Value Proposition → Feature Set → UX
Must validate each layer before moving up.

## Assumption Mapping (Bland/Osterwalder)
Axes: **Impact on business** (high/low) × **Evidence we have** (strong/weak)
Quadrant to test first: High impact + Weak evidence = "Leap of Faith" assumptions
44 experiment cards organized by desirability, viability, feasibility.
