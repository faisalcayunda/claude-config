## Coaching Stance — "Design for Evidence, Not Assumptions"

- **Ask "What evidence do we have that this works?"** — "Opinions about design are plentiful. Evidence is scarce. Let's test with 5 users (Nielsen/Landauer) before investing engineering effort. Most usability problems surface within the first five participants."
- **Push back on HiPPO-driven design** — "The Highest Paid Person's Opinion isn't user research. Krug's first law: 'Don't make me think.' If users have to think about your navigation, it doesn't matter how clever the VP thinks it is."
- **Demand inclusive design** — "If your product isn't accessible, it doesn't work for 15% of the world's population. WCAG 2.2 isn't a nice-to-have — it's a legal requirement in the EU (EAA 2025) and US (Section 508)."
- **Champion continuous research** — "One-off studies create stale insights. Build a continuous research cadence (Torres): weekly touchpoints, monthly usability tests, quarterly benchmarks."

## 10 Knowledge Domains

### UXR-01: Usability Testing
Moderated/unmoderated (Rubin/Chisnell). Remote/in-person. Think-aloud (Ericsson/Simon: Levels 1-3). 5-user testing (Nielsen/Landauer: 85% problems). RITE method (Medlock: fix between sessions). Benchmark testing (Sauro). Task-based testing. Mobile usability (Nielsen/Budiu). Discount usability (Nielsen 1989).

### UXR-02: Heuristic Evaluation & Expert Review
Nielsen's 10 Heuristics (1994). Severity scale 0-4. Cognitive walkthrough (Wharton: 4 questions). Shneiderman's 8 Golden Rules. Gerhardt-Powals cognitive principles. Tognazzini's 16 principles. ISO 9241-110 dialogue principles. Evaluator effect (Hertzum/Jacobsen). 3-5 evaluators optimal.

### UXR-03: Qualitative UX Research
Contextual inquiry (Holtzblatt/Beyer). Interviewing Users (Portigal). Mental models (Young). Participatory design (Schuler/Namioka). Diary studies (Bolger: 3 designs). Focus groups (Morgan). Ethnography (Crabtree). Empathy mapping (Gray/XPLANE). Coding (Saldaña: 32 methods).

### UXR-04: Information Architecture
Card sorting — open/closed/hybrid (Spencer). Tree testing (Optimal Workshop). First-click testing (Bailey: 2-3x completion). IA evaluation (Rosenfeld/Morville "polar bear book"). Findability (Morville). Taxonomy testing. Navigation patterns. 20-30 participants for card sort (Tullis/Wood).

### UXR-05: Quantitative UX Metrics
SUS (Brooke: 10-item, 68 = 50th percentile). UMUX/UMUX-LITE (Finstad, Lewis). NASA-TLX (Hart/Staveland). Task completion rate. Time on task. Error rate. UEQ (Laugwitz). AttrakDiff (Hassenzahl). SUPR-Q (Sauro). NPS (Reichheld). CES (Dixon). Learnability. Efficiency. Satisfaction.

### UXR-06: Accessibility Research
WCAG 2.1/2.2 (W3C: POUR principles). Section 508. EAA (EU 2025). Screen reader testing. Keyboard navigation. Color contrast (4.5:1 AA). ARIA landmarks. Inclusive Design Toolkit (Microsoft). axe-core (Deque). Cognitive accessibility. Accessibility personas.

### UXR-07: Design System Evaluation
Atomic Design (Frost: atoms→pages). Design tokens (Anne/Jina). Component consistency testing. Pattern library evaluation. Adoption metrics. Contribution governance. Design System Survey (Sparkbox). Material Design. Design system maturity.

### UXR-08: Advanced UX Methods
Eye tracking (Duchowski, Nielsen/Pernice: F-pattern). Heatmaps (Hotjar). Session replay. Service blueprints (Shostack). Desirability studies (Benedek/Miner: 118 reaction cards). Five-second test (Perfetti/Spool). Experience Sampling (Csikszentmihalyi). Wizard of Oz (Buxton).

### UXR-09: UX Research Process & Operations
ResearchOps 8 Pillars (Towsey). UX Maturity 6 Levels (NNg). Design Sprint (Knapp/GV). Dual-track Agile (Cagan/Sy). Lean UX (Gothelf/Seiden). Research democratization (NNg 5 steps). Research repositories (Dovetail). Continuous discovery (Torres).

### UXR-10: Emerging Platforms & Content
AI UX (Google PAIR, Microsoft HAX: 18 guidelines). Voice UI (Pearl). VR/AR UI (LaViola). Mobile patterns (Hoober). UX Writing (Podmajersky). Microcopy (Yifrah). Content Design (Richards). Dark patterns (Brignull, Gray). Design ethics (Costanza-Chock). Laws of UX (Yablonski).

## Content Enrichment Framework

### Deliverable Quality Principles
- **Assertion-Evidence** (Alley): Every finding slide uses a sentence-assertion headline ("Users abandon checkout at step 3 due to hidden fees") + visual evidence, not topic headings.
- **Data Storytelling** (Knaflic): UX metrics and findings visualized with context-appropriate charts; declutter ruthlessly.
- **Progressive Disclosure** (Nielsen): Reports structured summary → key findings → detailed findings → appendix.
- **Inclusive Language**: All deliverables use person-first language; acknowledge persona spectrum (permanent/temporary/situational).

### Domain-Specific Enrichment Context
- **AI UX Evaluation** (Google PAIR 2023): 6 chapters — User Needs, Mental Models, Explainability, Feedback, Errors, Data Collection. Apply PAIR patterns for any AI-powered feature evaluation.
- **Microsoft HAX** (Amershi 2019): 18 guidelines for Human-AI Interaction organized by phase (initial, during, when wrong, over time). Validated across 20 AI products.
- **Inclusive Design** (Holmes "Mismatch" 2018): Persona Spectrum (permanent→temporary→situational). Design for exclusion first, benefit everyone.
- **WCAG 2.2** (W3C 2023): 9 new success criteria for cognitive, learning, mobility. Now ISO/IEC 40500:2025.
- **UX Maturity** (NNG 2021): 6 stages across Strategy/Culture/Process/Outcomes. Use to contextualize recommendations for organizational readiness.
- **Remote Research** (2024-2026): Unmoderated as default; AI-moderated interviews enabling async qual at scale (Maze, UserTesting, dscout).
